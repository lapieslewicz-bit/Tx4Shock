Phase 1

Scene Size-Up

Big decisions that affect the entire call

Step 01
Is it SAFE?
BSI / scene safety
+

Scene safety checklist

  • Don BSI (gloves, mask, eye protection as needed)
  • Scan for hazards: traffic, downed wires, fire, violence, unstable structures
  • Identify potential for violence — trust your instincts
  • If unsafe, do NOT enter — stage and wait for police/fire
  • Re-evaluate safety throughout the call
Step 02
Do I need HELP?
Call early — before you need it
+

Call for additional resources

  • Police — violence, crime scene, crowd control
  • ALS — critical patient, ALS interventions needed
  • Fire Dept — entrapment, structural collapse, fire
  • Extrication — patient trapped in vehicle/machinery
  • Haz Mat — chemical, biological, radiological exposure
Call early. It is much easier to cancel help you don't need than to wait for help that's delayed.
Step 03
How many PATIENTS?
Triage if multiple
+

Patient count

  • Quickly scan the scene — look beyond the obvious
  • If multiple patients → enter Triage Mode (START triage)
  • Call for mutual aid immediately if overwhelmed
  • Document number of patients for dispatch
Step 04
OBSERVE the patient
MOI (c-spine?) or NOI
+

Mechanism / Nature of illness

  • MOI — Mechanism of Injury: how did the trauma occur? Assess c-spine risk.
  • NOI — Nature of Illness: what is the medical problem?
  • High MOI = high index of suspicion for hidden injuries
  • Get a general sense of problems and priorities from your first look
Phase 2

Primary Assessment

What immediate actions keep the patient alive?

General Impression & Chief Complaint
Your first look at the patient — get a sense of problems and priorities
+

General impression

  • Assess from the doorway: age, sex, level of distress
  • Is the patient sick-looking (ill, pale, diaphoretic)?
  • Position of comfort or distress?
  • Chief complaint: "What's the matter today?" in patient's own words
  • Does the environment give clues (medications, medical equipment, injury scene)?
↓ HOW SICK IS YOUR PATIENT? ↓
Crashing / Unstable
Load and go immediately
+

Crashing patient

  • Unresponsive / agonal breathing
  • Severe respiratory distress
  • Uncontrolled major hemorrhage
  • Signs of shock (pale, diaphoretic, BP dropping)
Action: Load and go — stabilize en route. Perform only life-saving interventions on scene.
Time Sensitive Issue
MI / Stroke — transport promptly
+

Time-sensitive conditions

  • MI: chest pain, diaphoresis, radiation to arm/jaw
  • Stroke (BE-FAST): Balance, Eyes, Face droop, Arm drift, Speech, Time
  • Sepsis: fever/hypothermia, altered mentation, hypotension
  • Severe hypoglycemia, Eclampsia
Action: Transport promptly. Limited scene time. Notify receiving hospital early.
Stable / Not Time Sensitive
Secondary assessment on scene
+

Stable patient

  • Alert, talking, normal skin signs
  • Adequate airway, breathing, and circulation
  • No immediate life threat identified
Action: Perform thorough secondary assessment on scene before or during transport.
NOTE — AHA C-A-B approach: When patient appears lifeless, prioritize Circulation before Airway and Breathing.

Differential Diagnosis
Develop a mental list of causes → rule in/out → match protocol → call medical direction if needed
+

Differential diagnosis process

  • Generate list of possible conditions based on presentation
  • Use history, exam findings, and vitals to rule each in or out
  • Match suspected condition to appropriate protocol
  • Call Medical Direction if outside protocol or uncertain
  • Communicate findings to receiving hospital
